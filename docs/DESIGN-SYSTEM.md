# Orion — design system

Orion is a sans-serif family drawn for streaming interfaces: television first, then desktop, tablet and phone.
This document records the design decisions, the glyph rules that follow from them, and the type presets for each platform.

## 1. Design brief and position

| Goal | Decision |
|---|---|
| Premium, cinematic, modern | Geometric skeleton (round O, circular bowls, straight-sided stems), calm rhythm, low stroke contrast |
| Readable at 3 m | Humanist corrections: open apertures, tall x-height (0.746 of cap height at text size), generous spacing at small optical sizes |
| One family for every surface | Four continuous axes: weight, width, optical size, grade |
| Unambiguous metadata | Differentiated I l 1, O 0, S 5, B 8, 6 9; tabular figures; a colon that centres on figures |
| Original | Every outline is generated by this project's own parametric code. Reference families were measured, never traced |

The blend is roughly two thirds geometric to one third humanist. Geometric: superelliptic rounds, near-monoline strokes,
single-arc shoulders, a circular `o`. Humanist: two-storey `a`, open `c e s` apertures, a tailed `l`, slightly narrower
`n h u` than a pure geometric design would give, and stroke thinning where arches meet stems.

### What was learned from the references (measurement only)

| Reference | Role in the brief | What was measured | How Orion responds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gotham | Primary aesthetic | cap 700, x-height 517 (Book), O 714 wide, stem 79; kerning AV −42…−100 | Same cap height; x-height 522; O 692 (slightly narrower for UI economy); Regular stem 88 for dark-UI robustness |
| Inter | UI precision | cap 727, x-height 546, stem 93; opsz axis; tabular figures, case forms, slashed zero | Same feature set for interface work (tnum, zero, case, frac, calt colon); x-height kept lower than Inter to stay elegant in display |
| Roboto Flex | Variable engineering | wght / wdth / opsz / GRAD axes, grade without reflow | Same four registered or conventional axes; GRAD is metrics-invariant by construction |
| Netflix Sans | Entertainment context | cap 696, x-height 512, stem 95; compact display, robust UI weights | Display optical size tightens spacing by about 5 %; Medium and SemiBold tuned as the UI workhorses |

Numbers for all four families are in `qa/reports/reference-metrics.json`; the side-by-side table is rendered in
`specimens/comparison/reference-metrics.png`. No reference outline is stored in this project.

## 2. Signature details

1. **Superelliptic rounds.** Curves use tangent handles with tension 0.574–0.612 instead of the circular 0.552, so bowls are a
   little squarer than a circle. They hold their shape on low-resolution panels and give counters more area.
2. **Leaning terminals.** Open terminals of `c e s a C G S 2 3 5 6 9` are cut by a straight face that leans 18° from vertical.
   In heavy weights the face rotates toward the stroke normal so that apertures stay open.
3. **Squircle dots.** Dots, periods and diacritic dots are rounded squares (tension 0.74), larger than stem width, so `i j ä ö ü` and
   punctuation survive video compression and distance.
4. **Tailed `l`, flagged `1`, plain `I`.** The three most confused characters in interface text have three different silhouettes.
   `ss01` adds serifs to `I`; `ss02` removes the tail of `l` for a more neutral texture.
5. **One hook rule.** The turns of `f j t l J` come from a single primitive whose radius adapts to stroke weight, so the family keeps one rhythm from Thin to Black.

## 3. Glyph construction rules

- **Vertical proportions (text size, units of 1000):** cap height 700, x-height 522, ascender 742, descender −204, figure height 700,
  overshoot 11. x-height rises to 530 at optical size 10 and falls to 510 at 72.
- **Stroke:** in `O`, the horizontal stroke is 86 % of the vertical stroke in Regular and 75 % in Black. Arches thin where they
  enter a stem and are buried slightly inside it to avoid dark joins.
- **Round-to-flat compensation:** rounds are about 3 % heavier than stems (O 91, I 88 in Regular) and overshoot both alignment lines by 11 units.
- **Diagonals** (`v w x y k A V W X Y K`) are thinned by weight-dependent factors and their vertices are cut flat.
- **Capitals** follow classical width groups softened for UI: `O Q C G D` wide (O is 692 in Regular), `E F L` 0.62–0.67 of `O`, `B P R S` 0.64–0.76.
- **Figures** are cap height, with a common advance for all figures except `1` in proportional mode and a single advance in
  tabular mode (`tnum`). The default zero is plain; `zero` gives a slashed form.
- **Punctuation** uses a dot about 1.3 × stem width (113 against 88 in Regular). Dashes, brackets, guillemets, `¿ ¡` and `@` have capital-height
  alternates reached through `case`.
- **Diacritics** are attached by anchors (`mark`, `mkmk`) and also shipped precomposed. Capital accents are flatter (`.case` forms).
  Vietnamese double accents are drawn as designed stacks, not as two unrelated marks.
- **Greek and Cyrillic** reuse Latin structures where the scripts share them (`Α Β Ε … / А В Е …`) and are drawn natively elsewhere
  with the same stroke rules. Serbian `б` has a localised form (`locl`).

### Confusable pairs

| Pair | Differentiation |
|---|---|
| I l 1 | plain stem / stem with right tail / stem with flag and no base |
| O 0 | 0 is 30 % narrower than O (483 against 692); slashed option through `zero` |
| S 5 | 5 has a flat top bar and a vertical neck |
| B 8 | 8 has no straight side and a smaller upper bowl; B has a full-height stem |
| 6 9 | open single-arc stems, not rotations of a closed spiral |
| rn m | `r` has a short arm and extra right-side space; the pair `r n` is kerned open |
| c e | `e` bar is horizontal and high; `c` aperture is wider |

Measured separation under simulated blur is reported in ACCESSIBILITY.md. `rn | m` is the weakest pair and is documented there.

## 4. Spacing and kerning

- Sidebearings come from stroke-shape classes (straight, round, diagonal, open) and are tied to counter width:
  Regular `n` has 72 / 67 units, `o` 44 / 44, `H` 86 / 86.
- Optical size changes spacing: +17 % at opsz 10, −22 % at opsz 72 relative to opsz 14 (sidebearings of `n`).
- Kerning is class-based and generated per master from glyph edge profiles: about 8,200 pairs per master, 206 right classes and
  185 left classes. Regular values: `AV` −72, `To` −72, `LT` −100, `r.` −77, `rn` +12. Kerning interpolates with the outlines and is
  identical across the grade axis.
- There are no ligatures. Interface text is frequently letter-spaced and no pair in Orion collides (2,704 letter pairs are
  checked for collisions at 8 extreme designspace locations in `qa/qa_interpolation.py`).

### Rules for using the presets

1. **Set `opsz` to the rendered size** (capped to 10–72). On the web `font-optical-sizing: auto` does this; native platforms need it set explicitly.
2. **Focus with grade, not weight.** Raising `GRAD` by 60–80 on the focused item darkens it without changing its width, so rails never reflow.
3. **Over video, add grade and a scrim.** Subtitles use `GRAD +40` on a 75 % black box; hero titles sit on a gradient scrim.
4. **On OLED dark UI, body text may use `GRAD −20`** to counter halation of light text on black.
5. **Use Condensed (`wdth 75–90`) for dense rails and long localisations** (German, Vietnamese, Russian labels run 20–35 % longer than English) instead of shrinking type below the minimum size.
6. **Metadata and timers use `tnum`.** Between tabular figures the colon is raised automatically (`calt`).
7. **Never go below the minimum size column on television.** The 24 px floor corresponds to roughly 8 arc-minutes of x-height at 3 m on a 55-inch panel.
